A Narrow Escape

Last Friday, I was attending a crucial interview on the top floor of a downtown skyscraper. Halfway through our discussion, the fire alarm suddenly shrilled through the corridors. It was obvious that something went terribly wrong downstairs. Everyone panicked instantly, because it would be fatal for us to take the crowded elevator in such an emergency.

There was scarcely time to deliberate. I was anxious to locate the emergency exit, while my interviewer shouted, "I feel sure that the roof is our safest bet!" We dashed toward the stairwell, but thick, acrid smoke was already billowing up like a monstrous wave. Realizing it was too late to descend, we had no choice but to rush upward to the helipad. Fortunately, the alarm turned out to be nothing more than a burnt piece of toast in the canteen, but I was deeply grateful for escaping the choking haze with my life!

Mr. Harrison, who lives across the hallway, has always been regarded as an eccentric recluse. It is altogether fitting and proper that people in our neighborhood mind their own business, yet his peculiar habits constantly aroused our curiosity. They assure me that he spent four decades working as an undercover cryptographer, which explains why he never leaves home before dusk.

Yesterday morning, now was the time to resolve the mystery once and for all. A piercing shriek came from his flat, followed by the clatter of shattered porcelain. We called on him to open the door immediately, warning that it was necessary that the police be notified. Just as we prepared to kick the latch open, the door creaked. There stood Mr. Harrison in an apron, smiling sheepishly. "I am proud to announce," he whispered triumphantly, "that my experimental robotic vacuum has finally learned how to catch mice—though at the expense of my grandmother's antique teapot!"

I designed to surprise my wife on our tenth anniversary by baking an exquisite three-tiered chocolate cake. Although my culinary expertise was next to nothing, I endeavored to follow every intricate instruction in the cookbook. I hoped that my sincere effort would compensate for my lack of flair, and I felt sure that she would be moved to tears.

However, things went awry almost from the outset. In my haste, I mistook a jar of baking soda for powdered sugar. When the mixture began to hiss and bubble like a volcanic crater, there was scarcely time to extinguish the oven flame before a deafening pop rattled the kitchen windows. Black batter was splattered across the ceiling. It is no wonder that my wife burst into laughter the moment she walked in. Looking at the charcoal-like lump on the counter, I sighed, "I do believe that reserving a table at an Italian bistro is a far wiser course of action!"

Yet, it goes without saying that nobody expects to find a full-grown stag reclining on their living room sofa. Last Sunday, I happened to return home earlier than usual. To my utter astonishment, the sliding glass door was unlatched, and a majestic creature with sweeping antlers was placidly chewing on my velvet cushions.

I made up my mind not to startle the beast, knowing that a panicked animal of that stature could reduce my sitting room to splinters in seconds. I was on the point of dialing animal control when the stag turned its head, blinked lazily, and sneezed. Taking advantage of the moment, I quietly tossed an apple onto the lawn outside. It was gratifying to see that the gentle giant was instantly tempted. He stood up majestically, trotted out into the sunlight, and left me with a pile of velvet fluff and an unbelievable story to tell!
